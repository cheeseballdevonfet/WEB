# What award-winning websites in 2025–Oct 2026 actually do, interaction by interaction

Research date: 2026-10-06. Scope: Jan 2025 to Oct 2026. Anything older is labelled "(older)".
Method note: Codrops (tympanus.net) returns a Cloudflare challenge to direct fetches. Codrops pages below were read through the r.jina.ai reader proxy and are cited by their original Codrops URLs. Awwwards "site" pages were fetched directly. They give jury scores, tags and the "highlighted elements" labels the jury/studio attached; they do not give prose descriptions of interactions.

## 1. Judging: what Awwwards, FWA, CSSDA actually score, and what jurors reward or penalise

### Takeaway
Awwwards weights Design 40 / Usability 30 / Creativity 20 / Content 10. It runs a separate Developer Award scored on six technical sub-criteria. In the 18 recent SOTD/SOTM pages sampled, winners score highest on creativity and on "Animations/Transitions" (about 8.5/10 on average). Their weakest areas are usability and accessibility (about 7.0/10 on average). Jurors' public writing says frame rate on real mid-range phones, load time, mobile behaviour and a reduced-motion path decide close calls. FWA and CSSDA publish less detail: FWA rewards "cutting edge creativity", and CSSDA scores UI, UX and Innovation.

**Juror and critic statements (2025–26)**
- Awwwards juror Hon Tran (two-time Independent of the Year nominee) contrasts a "nice" site with an award-winner. Art direction: "polished template" vs "specific point of view". Motion: "effects bolted on" vs "choreographed, meaningful". Performance: "dies under load" vs "~60fps on mid-range mobile". Accessibility: "often ignored" vs "reduced-motion path built in". His three principles: art direction serves a single idea; "Motion has a director, not just a library"; "Beauty at 60fps is the whole discipline". He says sites that drop frames on mid-range Android or take 6+ s to paint "will not win — jurors test on real devices". — [hontran.dev, 2026](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/best-award-winning-websites-2026)
- Utsubo (Jocelyn Lecamus, 5 Mar 2026), a studio guide that is not an official rubric, says:
  - Judges expect sub-3 s loads, 60fps animation and no layout shift. "Beautiful sites that take 5+ seconds to load" lose.
  - "Judges check on mobile. A desktop masterpiece that breaks on iPhone loses immediately."
  - Contrast, keyboard navigation and screen-reader support are checked, and Core Web Vitals (LCP, CLS, INP) affect usability scores.
  - "Lorem ipsum on a submitted site signals lack of content strategy."
  - FWA "tends to reward unconventional, experimental work". CSSDA scores "UI, UX, and Innovation", and Website of the Day needs average judge scores above 8.00. — [Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/award-winning-website-design-guide)
- FWA: "500+ Jury (200+ men and 200+ women) from 35+ Countries" vote daily. Awards are FWA of the Day, FWA of the Month, FWA of the Year, plus a public People's Choice. Its mission is to "showcase cutting edge creativity, regardless of the medium or technology used", favouring "progressive and forward thinking projects". The fetched page gives no scoring scale. — [FWA About](https://thefwa.com/about/about-fwa/)
- Critic view (Hacker News, 9–10 Jun 2025): Awwwards is "an art gallery of interesting, atypical and normally impractical and/or bad designs". The same commenter claims "scrolljacking and replacing the cursor seem to appear on well more than half of the Awwwards site" (his estimate, not measured). Another: "Flashiness is way more important than performance there, be it in UX, conversions or load times." A rebuttal: the aesthetic does leak into "your average corporate site". — [HN thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44221729)

### Inferences
- Usability was the lowest of the four design categories in all 18 sampled SOTD pages (range 7.12–7.9). Creativity was the highest in 13 of 18. Winners clear the bar on art direction and invention and lose points on usability. A prompt kit can win on usability without giving up spectacle: visible nav, a skip-intro path, and no scroll traps.
- In the 17 sampled Developer breakdowns, Accessibility averaged about 7.0 (range 6.0–7.6) and Animations/Transitions about 8.5 (range 7.8–9.4). Accessibility is the most common weak spot, even on SOTY-level work. Reduced-motion paths, focus states and semantic markup are an underused way to stand out.
- Content is only 10% of the Awwwards score. Still, two of the strongest Content scores (Tracing Art 8.09, Renaissance Edition 8.09) belong to sites built on real data or real product news. Every prompt in the kit should require real copy and data.

### Gaps
- Awwwards' full "Developer Guidelines" text (definitions of WPO, Semantics, etc.) was not on the fetched evaluation page.
- I found no official, published FWA scoring scale. I also found no primary CSSDA rubric page. A search snippet claimed "20 votes" and "average above 6", but I did not verify it on a fetched page, so the CSSDA details rest on Utsubo's secondary account.
- I did not find a published Awwwards SOTY 2025 jury commentary or blog post explaining why Lando Norris won. The only evidence is vote counts and SOTD scores.

## 2. Catalogue: 2025–Oct 2026 award-winning or celebrated sites

### Takeaway
Winners split into a few families:
- Cinematic brand or person sites: Lando Norris, Cartier, GQ & AP.
- Playable 3D worlds or games as portfolios: Bruno Simon, Messenger, Aurel, Ponpon Mania.
- Editorial or studio sites with type-led motion and inventive galleries: Siena, Kononenko, Immersive Garden, MindMarket.
- Product stories that dramatise a mundane object or abstract service: Oryzo, Cerebrium, Terminal Industries, Floema.

Nearly all use GSAP. Most add Lenis plus Three.js, OGL or Rive. Webflow is increasingly the host even for SOTY-level work.

## 3. Recurring patterns by component family, and fresh vs. cliché in 2026

### Takeaway
What is fresh in 2025–26:
- Interactions with physical metaphors and tactile feedback: tear, hold-to-break, drag-with-momentum, haptics, spring snaps.
- Shared or social presence: live cursors, multiplayer, global counters.
- Shaders as material: ink reveals, Fresnel shields, video ripple with momentum.
- Galleries users can reorganise.
- Layout morphs and transitions that can be interrupted.

What reads as stock in 2026:
- Generic fade-up-on-every-element.
- Cursor replacement and scrolljacking used by default.
- 0→100 counters with no idea behind them.
- Card grids with uniform radius.
- Cream + serif or dark + acid-green palettes used without a reason. These are now explicitly named as AI defaults, even though several winners use them.

A prompt should require a reason for the palette, tied to brand or subject, rather than banning colours outright.
- Fresh in 2026, by repeated appearance in recent winners or Codrops features:
  - Physical-metaphor controls (hold-to-break, tear, dial, cassette).
  - Social/multiplayer presence.
  - Interruptible ("spammable") morphs.
  - Multi-mode galleries users can rearrange.
  - Shader-as-material image treatments.
  - Haptics.
  - Sound as part of interaction design.
  - 404 and footer as designed moments.
- Stock by 2026, based on critiques and the AI-tell lists rather than a jury source:
  - Fade-up-everything.
  - Default cursor replacement.
  - Scrolljacking.
  - Counter preloaders that exist only to fill time (still used, e.g. 1Minus1).
  - Uniform card grids.
  - Eyebrow labels with 01/02/03 numbering.
- Horizontal-scroll galleries (ERA, Aug 2026) and marquees (House of Yellow's four-marquee play button) still appear in 2026 winners. Used in a contained, specific way they are not penalised. Used as the default section layout they read as template.

- I did not measure how often smooth scroll (Lenis) appears among winners. Qualitatively it appears in most case studies fetched (Lando, TrueKind, Aurel, Kononenko, Yestalgia, House of Yellow, bleibtgleich).

## 4. What separates a "million-dollar" site from a competent template

### Takeaway
The gap is direction and finish, not technology:
- One art-directed idea carried through every component.
- Motion choreographed with named easings and durations, and interruptible.
- Restraint, with quiet sections framing a few big moments.
- Obsessive state and QA work.
- Hard performance budgets with adaptive quality tiers.
- Real content.

### Cited Findings
- Award-winner vs nice site: a "specific point of view", motion that is "choreographed, meaningful", "~60fps on mid-range mobile", a "reduced-motion path built in". Art direction "must serve a single idea". Unseen Studio: "restraint reads as premium". Obys: "static frames already look like posters". — [hontran.dev](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/best-award-winning-websites-2026)
- Restraint and pacing:
  - Dropbox: "not every section needed dramatic moments" and "The story only works when every part moves together". — [Awwwards case study](https://www.awwwards.com/case-study-dropbox-brand-guidelines.html)
  - Kononenko: "A strong digital experience does not necessarily come from making every interaction technically complicated", with simplicity around complex moments. — [Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/09/18/kononenko-architectural-bureau/)
  - TrueKind: type carries "the creative weight"; "Too much movement can be overwhelming." — [Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/06/25/designing-truekind-a-skincare-brands-journey-through-moodboards-motion-and-meaning/)
  - Dash: "Interaction works best when it supports the concept rather than drawing attention to itself." — [Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/07/21/magnetic-commerce-building-the-dash-creative-website/)
- Craft details:
  - Dropbox tracked "over 200" QA issues covering timing, breakpoints and performance; hover collisions were engineered away on colour chips. — [Awwwards case study](https://www.awwwards.com/case-study-dropbox-brand-guidelines.html)
  - Kononenko made all interactions "spammable", so they stay responsive when interrupted mid-animation. — [Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/09/18/kononenko-architectural-bureau/)
  - Vanderpotte uses "vocal prototyping" with "mouth sound effects—whooshes, clicks, and snaps" and tunes easings "shoulder-to-shoulder with a designer". — [Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/05/27/whooshes-snaps-and-shaders-adrien-vanderpotte-and-the-feeling-of-the-interface/)
  - Aurel specifies exact durations and easings per transition. — [Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/05/20/behind-the-curtain-building-aurels-grand-theater-from-design-to-code/)
  - Yestalgia avoids layout jump on expand and reduces split granularity on mobile. — [Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/09/12/yestalgia-bringing-decathlons-90s-spirit-to-life-through-a-playful-digital-experience/)
- Performance budgets:
  - ZERO: over 1 GB of sources to under 10 MB shipped, 60fps on budget Android, downgrade above 22 ms / upgrade below 12 ms, "Asset preparation and GPU uploads deserve as much attention as rendering itself". — [Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/07/17/zero-the-engineering-behind-a-defiant-interactive-narrative/)
  - Bruno Simon: an automatic mobile preset plus compressed GPU textures. — [Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/brunos-portfolio-case-study.html)
  - Aurel: "Every polygon matters", plus adaptive tick rates. — [Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/05/20/behind-the-curtain-building-aurels-grand-theater-from-design-to-code/)
  - Utsubo: sub-3 s load, 60fps, no CLS. — [Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/award-winning-website-design-guide)
- Content and maintainability:
  - Yestalgia built "every section of the site [as] a Blockstudio block" so client edits can't break art direction. — [Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/09/12/yestalgia-bringing-decathlons-90s-spirit-to-life-through-a-playful-digital-experience/)
  - "Lorem ipsum… signals lack of content strategy". — [Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/award-winning-website-design-guide)
- Time investment: Aurel took about 2 years to find the art direction plus about 1 year to build; Bruno Simon "a little over a year"; Kononenko 8 months. — [Codrops Aurel](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/05/20/behind-the-curtain-building-aurels-grand-theater-from-design-to-code/); [Awwwards Bruno](https://www.awwwards.com/brunos-portfolio-case-study.html); [Codrops Kononenko](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/09/18/kononenko-architectural-bureau/)

### Inferences
- For prompt writing, "million-dollar" means each component prompt should specify all of the following:
  - The concept the interaction expresses (e.g. "magnetic", "theatre", "cassette").
  - Exact trigger → response → duration → easing.
  - Interrupt behaviour.
  - Mobile and touch variant.
  - Reduced-motion variant.
  - A performance budget or tiering rule.
  - Real content.

  Template sites leave these unspecified. That gap is exactly where AI output defaults.

### Gaps
- No published byte or JS budgets for Lando Norris or other SOTY-level sites were found, beyond qualitative "lazy-loading" claims.

## 5. Using AI (Claude, v0, Lovable, etc.) to produce award-level sites: what works, what fails, the tells

### Takeaway
By late 2026 AI can write all the code for a Codrops-featured portfolio and reach "Muzli Picks" level demos. That happens only when a human supplies the art direction: references, exact states, named bans and taste checks. Left alone, models fall back to Inter, indigo/purple gradients, uniform cards and fade-ins. Brand fidelity and how motion feels "in the hand" still need human review.

### Cited Findings
- bleibtgleich'26 (Codrops, 23 Sep 2026): "Absolutely all of the code was written by Claude" (via the Claude Agent SDK, with ChatGPT also used). A live multiplayer cursor and chat system worked "on the first try". Manual work was needed for dead-cursor cleanup, merging multi-line text filters and the dial's feel after colleague testing. Method: give Claude specific effects with references, and ask for artifacts with tweakable variables rather than production code. — [Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/09/23/bleibtgleich26-a-180-turn-from-brutalism-to-minimalism/)
- Muzli (Petras Baukys, 28 Sep 2026) on Claude Opus 5.5:
  - Demos:
    - Breton sea-salt site: WebGL water hero that ripples with the cursor; a draining tide on scroll reveals salt crystals.
    - 12 s kinetic title sequence synced to 120 BPM.
    - Spring-physics music player with 1:1 finger tracking and velocity-aware dismiss.
  - The prompt recipe includes:
    - Audience.
    - A mood with anti-patterns ("not beach kitsch").
    - "One memorable hero moment".
    - Section sequence and motion libraries.
    - Phone layout and reduced motion.
    - Explicit bans: "Do not use: cream background, italic accent words, 01/02/03 labels".
    - "ask for ten options, then remix".
  - Failures:
    - "used the wrong logo, replaced Every's green with dark purple, added its own background art".
    - Without bans it "falls back on… Inter fonts, purple gradients, minimal animations".
    - Spring curves needed manual tuning.
    - "taste is still the bottleneck".
  - The model built 8 type pairings before choosing one. — [Muzli](https://muz.li/blog/claude-opus-5-5-for-designers/)
- AI tells (925studios, 19 Mar 2026):
  - Inter.
  - "Purple-to-blue gradients".
  - Vague copy ("Build the future of work", "Scale without limits").
  - Stock "diverse group… laptop" images or "abstract 3D blobs".
  - "same 16px border radius and 24px padding" everywhere.
  - "Generic fade-in on every element".
  - Fixes: a display font, a semantic colour system, real photos, motion tied to state changes, founder-voice copy. — [925studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-web-design-guide)
- Root-cause explanation (dev.to, 25 Mar 2026): Tailwind UI's indigo-500 default seeded training data. "Its not a preference. Its a probability distribution." Tells: gradient-text heroes, three-column icon feature grids, 0.5rem radii, low-opacity shadows, purple CTAs. Fixes: hex palettes, named fonts, described layouts, reference sites. — [dev.to](https://dev.to/alanwest/why-every-ai-built-website-looks-the-same-blame-tailwinds-indigo-500-3h2p)
- Anthropic's own frontend-design skill lists five AI-default clusters to avoid (see §3). It also tells the model to "Choose your typefaces deliberately", to use at most 1–2 families, and to avoid "Accenting just a single word or phrase in a headline". — [anthropics/skills](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md)
- Tool-level critique: Cerebrium's team (human-built) tried WebGPU/TSL and reverted over a ~20 s first frame. This is a reminder that new-tech choices need verification, which AI defaults may skip. — [Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/07/23/building-cerebrium-making-serverless-infrastructure-tangible/)

### Inferences
- What works with AI: tight, referenced, single-effect prompts with tweakable parameters, explicit bans, exact states and timings, mobile and reduced-motion requirements, and iteration on many options.
- What fails: brand fidelity, motion feel and physics tuning, performance on real devices, and taste or selection. The prompt kit should build in human or automated checks for these: device testing, an FPS budget, a reduced-motion pass and a brand-asset lock.
- The tells now go beyond indigo gradients. Cream + serif, dark + acid accent and eyebrow/numbering chrome are also flagged as AI defaults. A kit should force a stated reason for each visual choice rather than swap one default for another.

### Gaps
- I found no 2025–26 site documented as winning an Awwwards SOTD that was primarily generated by v0 or Lovable. I also found no juror statement on how AI-generated submissions are judged.
- I found no primary v0 or Lovable vendor writing about award-level output. Comparative critiques found in search were tool reviews, not fetched.
